01 — Overview

The short version

Brand identity and product design system for a B2B fintech platform that gives small business owners one readable view of their money.

Brand and product, solo

Ramp, Plaid, Mercury, Brex, Qonto
02 — The premise

Small business owners don’t lack financial data. They lack a reading of it.

Money arrives from Stripe, PayPal, card refunds. It leaves through vendor subscriptions nobody re-reads. Somewhere between those two flows sits a runway number most founders discover a quarter too late — not because it was hidden, but because it was never composed into anything legible.

Peak Finance Labs began as a question I wanted to answer for myself: what would a financial command centre look like if it were built to be read at a glance rather than merely displayed — and if it refused every convention the category defaults to?

03 — Constraints

Five rules, written before the first sketch

01 The name is too long for a wordmark. So the mark has to carry the brand on its own — at 24px in a browser tab as readily as on a wall.
02 One primary logo. No alternates to hide behind. A single lockup forces the mark to be right rather than merely flexible.
03 White space is structure, not padding. Financial density is unavoidable. Breathing room is what makes it survivable.
04 No generic fintech blue. Ramp, Plaid, Mercury, Brex, Qonto — the whole category lives in one cold palette. Sitting inside it means disappearing into it.
05 Legible to anyone. A founder reading it at 11pm, not a designer reading it in Figma.
Growth
An identity built to scale and stay stable across every surface it lands on — without borrowing the category’s cold, interchangeable palette.
Trust
High contrast and strict hierarchy, so a cash position can be read with confidence in the two seconds someone actually gives it.
Analytical
A system that supports the next decision rather than reporting on the last one. Insight is surfaced, not buried in a table.
04 — The mark

A peak assembled out of data — not drawn as a mountain

The mark reads as a summit, a direct tie to the name. But it is built the way the product is: from discrete modular blocks that lock together. Nothing is drawn freehand. Every unit is the same square, rotated 45° and stepped, so the silhouette is a consequence of the system rather than an illustration sitting on top of it.

45°
Every edge in the mark runs at the same angle. The peak is where two of them intersect.
1×
The module never scales inside the mark. Density comes from count, never from size.
24px
Minimum legible size — tested at browser-tab scale before anything else was approved.
05 — Lockups

The mark leads. The wordmark follows

“Peak Finance Labs” is three words and nineteen characters — too long to work as a wordmark at any size that matters. So the identity inverts the usual hierarchy: the mark carries recognition, and the type exists to name it once you are already looking.

On dark and on brand-green the mark switches to a single flat tint — the stepped tonal version only survives on paper.
Clearspace on every side equals one module — the same square the mark is built from. It scales with the logo, so the rule never needs restating. Don’t stretch · don’t rotate · no effects · no mid-tones
06 — Colour

Warm, not cold. Rationed, not decorative

I stepped away from the cold blues every banking app defaults to. Warm neutrals reduce eye fatigue across a long audit session; ink and forest carry the grounded, premium weight; signal and alert are rationed hard, so the only things that raise their voice are the things that should.

08 — The product

Two screens. The two questions that actually get asked

“How much runway do I have?” and “where did it go?” Everything else in a finance tool is a variation on those two. So rather than sketch twelve screens shallowly, I built two properly — and let the system show through them.

01 Dashboard The state of the business in one screen.
02 Cash flow ledger Every movement, scannable vertically.
09 — Anatomy

The metric card does four things and stops

Label
DM Sans 14. Says what it is, never what to feel about it.
Value
Space Grotesk Bold 32. The only element allowed to be big.
Delta
JetBrains Mono 13 with an arrow. Forest for up, Alert for down.
Nothing else
No sparkline, no icon, no progress ring. Density is the enemy here.
The insight bar

The one element that speaks first rather than waiting to be read. It sits between the metrics and the chart because that is where a founder’s attention naturally lands after scanning the top row — and it always ends in an action, never in a statement. A number that has nothing to be done about it does not earn a bar.

The ledger row

Three zones, fixed positions, every row. The date holds the left edge so the eye never re-hunts for it; a single pipe separates counterparty from date, because table borders add ink without adding meaning; every amount is monospaced and right-aligned, so magnitude is readable as shape before it is read as a number.

10 — The system

A 12-column grid and a sidebar that never moves

The dark sidebar is not decoration — it is a hard boundary between navigation and data. Ink on the left, Paper on the right, so the eye never confuses “where I am” with “what I am looking at.” Every content block on the right snaps to the same twelve columns, which is why the metric row, the chart and the ledger all share one left edge.

11 — Brand into product

Four brand decisions, four interface consequences

Forest → Positive deltas and the cash-position line. Growth is literally the brand colour.
Ink → The sidebar. The brand’s heaviest tone becomes the wall between navigation and data.
JetBrains Mono → Every numeral, every tag, every timestamp. The typographic decision became the alignment system.
Paper → The page ground. Warmth chosen at the brand stage is what makes a long audit session survivable.

If a brand system can’t survive contact with a data table, it was decoration.

12 — Reflection

What I’d fix before this became real

01 The amber is a constraint, not a colour Signal reads at 2.14:1 on Paper. It survives only as fills, indicators and large numerals — and I would rather state that limit than quietly break it later.
02 Two screens is not a product There are no empty states, no failure states, no onboarding, and no mobile. What exists is a system with two proofs, not a shipped tool.
03 The insight bar needs a rule Right now it is one hand-written line. To be trustworthy it would need a threshold model — what counts as worth interrupting someone for.

The part I’d keep: deciding the palette and the type system before opening a single screen. Every interface choice that followed had somewhere to come from.
