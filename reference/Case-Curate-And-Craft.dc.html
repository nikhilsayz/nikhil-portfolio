02 — The mark

Two arcs that open into an M

Two concentric arcs read as C and C. The outer ring breaks at the upper right and folds inward to form the M’s apex, so the third letter is made from the gap rather than added beside it. Nothing overlaps — the counters stay open, which is what keeps the mark legible when it shrinks to a profile picture.

Stacked — reversed. The default for avatars and dark placements.
Stacked — positive. Same weights, no optical correction needed.
03 — Lockups

Two orientations, one set of proportions

Stacked for square spaces, horizontal for headers and signatures. In the horizontal lockup the name sets in three lines so its block matches the monogram’s height — the ampersand becomes “and” because a symbol at that size fights the mark’s own geometry.

Horizontal — positive. Name left-aligned to the monogram’s optical centre.
04 — Palette

Monochrome & sleek

Four steps, no hue. An agency’s mark appears next to client work constantly, and a colour of its own would clash with half of it. Black and white carry the logo; the two greys exist for supporting type and dividers.

05 — Decisions

Three calls behind the mark

01 Single stroke weight throughout Both arcs and the M share one line thickness, so the monogram reads as one drawn gesture rather than three letters assembled.
02 The break does the work Opening the outer ring at the top right creates the M without adding a shape. The negative space carries a letter, which is what makes the mark worth a second look.
03 No colour of its own Monochrome is a working constraint, not a mood. The mark has to sit beside client campaigns in every palette without arguing with them.
06 — Scope

What this is, and isn’t

My contribution here is the logo: the monogram, the two lockups and the monochrome palette it lives in. No brand guidelines, no collateral, no product work.

It is on the site because a mark that has to survive at avatar scale, in positive and reverse, with a single stroke weight, is a specific discipline — and it’s worth showing that discipline on its own rather than padding it into a case study it isn’t.
